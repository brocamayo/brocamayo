## 1. Hook formulas taught by top strategists (named formulas plus templates)

### Takeaway
The strongest and most consistent guidance, from YouTube's own Creator Liaison, the MrBeast memo, and Paddy Galloway, is not a clever formula. It is this: **the opening must immediately confirm the promise of the title and thumbnail, then get into the value with no preamble.** Named "formulas" (pattern interrupt → promise → open loop; hook → context → payoff; cold open) are vendor or blog packaging of that principle, and almost none come with controlled evidence.

### Cited Findings
**MrBeast "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" memo (leaked Sept 2024; authorship widely reported but not formally confirmed):**
- The memo names three metrics to track: click-through rate (CTR), average view duration (AVD) and average view percentage (AVP) — [Creator Handbook summary](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- Quoted: "the first minute has the most loss (go look). This is why we freak out so much about the first minute and go so above and beyond to make it the best we freakin can." — [Daniel Scrivner summary](https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/); [memo PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6623b7720b009050313e701c/66ede69453b7bbadcd2f05a8_How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production%20(2).pdf); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- The memo reportedly cites a video "where we lost 21 million viewers in the first minute of the video (which surprisingly compared to other channels is above average)" — [Scrivner](https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/mrbeast-leaked-pdf-spills-secrets-how-to-be-successful-on-youtube/)
- Structure: first minute (hook and match the click), minutes 1–3 ("crazy progression": cover a lot of ground fast, e.g. several days of a forest-survival challenge rather than only day one), and minutes 3–6 (a re-engagement point) — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/); [Scrivner](https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/)
- The title and thumbnail set expectations, and the video must deliver what they promise (for example, a promised scene must appear) — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)

**Paddy Galloway (strategist for MrBeast and others):**
- "22. Always plan your title and thumbnail before recording, everyone knows this, few do this. 23. Don't waste your viewers time in your intro, get into the value asap. 24. The best intro doesn't feel like an intro, it seamlessly flows into the body." — [Paddy Galloway on X, May 2024](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1788967959882346991)
- From his Shorts study (3.3B views, April 2023): "make your first second really punchy… Treat your intro like a thumbnail." — [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898373365051392?lang=en). This was about Shorts but is widely applied to long-form.
- His Editing Podcast appearance has chapters titled "Why The First 30 Seconds Are CRUCIAL" and "How To Create A Satisfying Intro" (content not retrievable) — [The Editing Podcast](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/the-editing-podcast/episodes/Paddy-Galloway-Tells-Us-How-To-Edit-YouTube-For-TV-e2cl1fe)

**YouTube's Creator Liaison (Rene Ritchie):**
- Titles and thumbnails are a promise, and the video must keep it, starting with a hook that reassures viewers the click was worth it — [YouTube Blog, Rene's Top Five, Jan 18 2024](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-january-18-2024/)
- Whatever the thumbnail promises, the video must deliver, or viewers feel misled and leave. The intro should reward the click right away and keep paying it off — [YouTube Blog, Mar 21 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-21-2024-edition/)
- His recipe: an idea with broad appeal, then a strong thumbnail and title, then a hook that grabs attention, then a video that fulfills the packaging — [YouTube Blog, Jul 19 2024](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-july-19-2024/)
- The opening can't just be an introduction. It has to pay off the viewer's interest — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)
- YouTube's Test & Compare picks thumbnail and title winners on watch time, not clicks alone. Title A/B testing is now available for long-form — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/); [Ritchie on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/reneritchie_did-you-know-that-you-can-now-ab-test-titles-activity-7380971030007386113-lr1g)

**Named formulas circulating among analytics vendors and strategists (no controlled evidence attached):**
- **Pattern interrupt → specific promise → open loop.** For long-form, extend it to 2–4 sentences: interrupt, promise, open loop, brief stakes — [Hypenest](https://hypenest.ai/tools/intro-hook-generator); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)
- **0–5s attention grab / 5–15s promise / 15–30s stakes or context, then any branding.** This is a common template across first-30-second guides — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video/); [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/first-30-seconds)
- **Cold open / best-clip-first / big reveal,** with intros cut entirely or kept under about 5 seconds — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video/)
- **BINGE framework (Social Media Examiner):** an opening hook, repeating micro-hooks at the start of each section, and an ending that builds momentum. Do not signal "wrapping up" early, because that cues exits — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)
- **"Anti-hook" (skepticism-first):** address the viewer's doubt within 10 seconds. "Hey guys" acts as an exit prompt for new viewers — [RetentionYT](https://retentionyt.com/anti-hook-beat-viewer-skepticism-10-seconds)
- **Fireship formula (third-party characterization):** "Hook, Punchline, Zoom, Repeat… The first three seconds state the problem in one line. No context, no backstory." — [CreatorOS](https://www.creatoros.ca/blog/fireship-style-video-editing-with-ai/)
- **Curiosity gap:** hint at the payoff without revealing it fully — [The Sunday Strategist](https://thesundaystrategist.beehiiv.com/p/the-1-mistake-you-re-making-with-your-thumbnails)
- **Write 2–3 intro versions,** then keep the one that holds best on the retention graph — [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-make-a-youtube-intro-that-keeps-viewers-watching); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)

### Inferences
- These **templates for the daily brief** are synthesized from the above, not quoted from any one source:
  1. **"Headline + Why it matters to your money" (confirm-click opener):** "[Specific news fact from the title], and here's what it means for [your 401(k) / your job / your AI tools] before you get to work." The title is confirmed in sentence one and the value is stated in sentence two.
  2. **"Number-first":** "[Concrete number: e.g., 'Nvidia moved X% overnight' / 'mortgage rates hit X%'] — [one-line why]. Three things you need to know this morning."
  3. **"Stakes + roadmap" (MrBeast-style crazy progression adapted to news):** the lead story in 1–2 lines, then "Plus: [teaser 2], and [teaser 3, the most curiosity-inducing, saved for later]." This is an open loop that pays off later in the video.
  4. **"Myth or skeptic answer" (anti-hook):** "You've probably seen headlines saying [X]. Here's what actually happened." This suits credibility-first finance content.
- The weekend tutorial should use **"Result first":** show the finished output or money outcome of the AI workflow in the first 10 seconds, then "I'll show you every step, including the part that broke."
- Because Galloway, Ritchie and the MrBeast memo agree, "title–thumbnail–hook alignment" should be treated as the **hard rule**, and specific formulas as interchangeable tactics.

### Gaps

Formulas often attributed to them (e.g., Film Booth's "hook → context → payoff/proof", Derral Eves' "ICE" or "HEAT" frameworks, Ali Abdaal's "hook, then intro, then content" with a short credibility line) **could not be verified this session** and should not be cited without checking.

### Takeaway
I found **no transcript-based analysis** of how these creators open, and no fetchable transcripts. - **Signature openers [UNVERIFIED – model memory; verify against videos before quoting]:**

## 3. Daily news-brief openings, and avoiding repetitive hooks

### Takeaway
The available evidence is mostly structural. Daily shows rely on a **fixed format ritual plus a variable headline hook**: a recognizable sign-on and date, then the day's most consequential or surprising story. DeFranco's history shows that format changes can confuse a daily audience, and that splitting risky single stories out keeps one item from sinking the whole episode.

### Cited Findings
- The Philip DeFranco Show uses a fast-paced format with frequent jump cuts. DeFranco lays out both sides of a story, then gives his opinion. "Today in Awesome" is a recurring closing segment — [Wikipedia: The Philip DeFranco Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philip_DeFranco_Show)
- In 2018 DeFranco tested releasing controversial single stories as separate morning videos so that "a single flagged story would not tank the entire show" — [Tubefilter, Aug 2018 (older)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2018/08/20/philip-defranco-show-new-format/)
- An earlier test splitting the daily show into several segments caused audience confusion, which led him to found SourceFed instead — [Wikipedia: SourceFed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SourceFed)
- Fireship's Code Report has a newsreel title card and a date — [De Programmatica Ipsum](https://deprogrammaticaipsum.com/fireship/)

### Inferences
- **Anti-repetition rules for a daily brief (synthesized):**
  1. Keep the **ritual** fixed (≤3s: show name and date), and make the **first content sentence** always new: the single most money-relevant fact of the day, stated with a number or a named entity.
  2. Rotate the hook *type* across the week: number-first, question, myth-correction, "what changed overnight", and "one thing to do today" (an educational action, not a trade).
  3. Never reuse generic superlatives ("huge day in AI"). The hook must be falsifiable and specific, or it reads as filler.
  4. Tease the most curiosity-inducing story as story #2 or #3 (an open loop) rather than leading with it every time.
  5. Following DeFranco: if one story is a potential policy or claim risk (e.g., a speculative market rumor), keep it out of the hook. Leading with an unverified claim puts the whole episode's credibility at risk.

### Gaps
- No sources found on how Morning Brew (Morning Brew Daily), TLDR News, Bloomberg Quicktake, Axios or Puck structure their video openings. No data on whether daily shows see higher or lower first-30-second retention than one-offs (returning-viewer share likely matters, but this is unverified).

## 4. Title–thumbnail–hook alignment ("confirm the click"), and rules for the first sentence

### Takeaway
YouTube's own definition of the "Intro" retention moment explicitly ties a good intro to **matching the title and thumbnail**. Ritchie, the MrBeast memo and Galloway all treat the packaging as a promise to pay off immediately. The first sentence should restate or advance the specific promise, never re-set it.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Studio defines Intro as "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds. A high intro percentage could mean: The content in the first 30 seconds matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title. The content kept the audience interested." — [YouTube Help: Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415) (quoted via search extract; page fetch blocked)
- If the thumbnail shows a price tag, mention that cost within 15 seconds. Practitioner advice — [The Creator Playbook](https://www.creator-playbook.com/articles/your-intro-sucks-fix-first-30-seconds)
- Plan the title and thumbnail before recording — [Galloway](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1788967959882346991)
- The intro should reward the click at once and keep paying it off. A thumbnail promise that isn't delivered makes viewers feel misled — [YouTube Blog, Mar 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-21-2024-edition/)
- Teaser-first openings (showing the payoff) "immediately confirm the viewer's click" — [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-make-your-youtube-intro-and-outro-irresistible-with-examples)
- Test & Compare optimizes on watch time, not CTR alone, so misleading packaging that gets clicks but loses viewers will lose tests — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)

### Inferences
- **First-sentence rules (synthesized):**
  1. It must contain the **key noun from the title** (the company, product, rate or number) within the first ~5 seconds.
  2. If the thumbnail shows a number, chart or face reaction, **say or show that element within 15 seconds**.
  3. Say why it matters to *this viewer* (money, job, tools) in sentence two.
  4. No restating the topic generically ("Today we're going to talk about…"). Advance it instead.
  5. Write the title, thumbnail text and first sentence **together as one unit** before filming the 6:50am brief.

### Gaps
- No public quantitative study isolating the effect of title–hook mismatch on retention. YouTube's Intro definition is the most authoritative statement available.

## 5. What to cut from intros; placement of the credential or intro line

### Takeaway
There is near-universal agreement to cut channel bumpers, "hey guys" openers, long self-introductions and any preamble before value. Credentials should come **after** the hook, as a one-line reason to trust you. Sponsor placement at the very top lacked direct evidence in my searches. There is a **real conflict**: many top serial and daily shows open with a short, fixed signature greeting and still succeed.

### Cited Findings
- "Don't waste your viewers time in your intro, get into the value asap." — [Galloway](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1788967959882346991)
- Cut long intros or keep them under about 5 seconds. Put any branded intro after the hook — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video/); [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/first-30-seconds)
- "Hey guys" is called an exit prompt for first-time viewers — [RetentionYT](https://retentionyt.com/anti-hook-beat-viewer-skepticism-10-seconds) — and a common mistake — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
- "Context (who you are, what the video is about) comes after the hook, not before." — [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-make-your-youtube-intro-and-outro-irresistible-with-examples)
- Cold open: a short hook segment *before* any logos or "Hi guys" — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-intro-be)
- Suggested intro lengths of 5–15 seconds, with the promise resolved by 15–20 seconds — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-intro-be); [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/youtube-video-hook-formulas)
- Graham Stephan's "in real estate" framing works as an embedded credential inside the hook rather than a separate intro — [Wono Strategy](https://typefully.com/wono_strategy/how-graham-stephan-made-3m-views-with-a-shitty-Br2FndW)

### Inferences
- **Conflict:** blanket "never say hey guys" (vendor blogs, no data) versus the fixed sign-ons of successful serial shows (DeFranco, Two Minute Papers, Fireship, Graham Stephan; unverified memory). The better-evidenced resolution, consistent with Galloway's "the best intro doesn't feel like an intro", is that **a greeting is not the problem; delay is**. A ≤2–3 second ritual fused with or following the hook is fine. A 10–20 second greeting plus self-intro plus "before we start, subscribe" is not.
- **Credential line:** one sentence, ≤5 seconds, placed after the confirm-click line (~10–20s). It should be relevant and non-promotional. Example: "I track AI and markets every morning so you don't have to — no stock picks, just what changed." This doubles as the no-recommendation disclaimer, which builds trust in finance content.
- **Sponsor reads:** put them after the first story is delivered (mid-roll), never before the hook. This is inferred from the "value first" consensus. No direct data was found.
- **"Subscribe" asks:** move them to after value is delivered (inferred).

### Gaps
- No data found on sponsor-read placement and early retention, or on the optimal timing of a credential line.

## 6. First-30-second retention data and benchmarks

### Takeaway
**YouTube itself publishes no universal benchmark.** It defines "Intro" as the % still watching at 30 seconds and says the first moments see the most loss. The commonly cited numbers (60–75% retained at 30s as "good"; ~55% lost by 60s; 23.7% average retention) all trace to vendor blogs or an unretrievable 2025 Retention Rabbit report. The single most concrete practitioner datum is MrBeast's memo: "the first minute has the most loss."

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's Intro metric = % still watching after 30 seconds — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- MrBeast memo: the first minute has the most loss. One video lost 21 million viewers in the first minute, "above average" compared with other channels — [Scrivner](https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/)
- vidIQ: aim to keep at least 60% past 30 seconds, with the strongest hooks holding over 65%, "and know that the platform average is far lower" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- Attributed to the 2025 Retention Rabbit benchmark (second-hand): average retention is 23.7%; intros holding over 65% correlate with 58% higher AVD; 55% of viewers are lost in the first 60 seconds; only ~16.8% of videos hold past 50% — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/retention-graphs-how-to-read-youtube-analytics-2026); [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-improve-audience-retention-on-youtube-top-tips). **Original report not retrieved.**
- Conflicting thresholds: below 70% at 30s means YouTube "punishes" the video (no source given) — [Creator Playbook](https://www.creator-playbook.com/articles/your-intro-sucks-fix-first-30-seconds); top performers keep 70%+ — [RSI](https://www.rsinc.com/what-is-the-30-second-rule-on-youtube.php); a 40%+ drop by 30s signals a weak hook — [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-retention-guide); over 33% leave in 30s with a weak intro — [Creator Playbook](https://www.creator-playbook.com/articles/your-intro-sucks-fix-first-30-seconds)
- A vendor table puts typical 30-second retention for education and tutorial content at 60–75% (unverified) — via [Social Media Examiner / socialrails search extract](https://socialrails.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-complete-guide)
- Shorts: the best-performing Shorts hold 70–90% on Viewed vs Swiped Away (Galloway's 3.3B-view study, 2023) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/); [Galloway](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315)
- Claims that YouTube "penalizes" early drop-off are blog assertions, not confirmed by YouTube documentation — [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-improve-audience-retention-on-youtube-top-tips)

### Inferences
- A workable **target** for the brief: ≥65% at 30s (aspirational), ≥55–60% (acceptable). Treat these as **directional**, from vendor data, and benchmark against the channel's own trailing-10-video median rather than industry numbers.
- The data the creator should actually track: Studio Intro % for every episode, logged alongside the hook type used (see section 3 rotation), to build a channel-specific evidence base within 4–6 weeks.

### Gaps
- No YouTube-published industry-wide 30-second benchmark. No Creator Insider statement retrieved. The Retention Rabbit methodology and sample are unknown.

## 7. Hooks for 30-minute videos versus 6–10 minute videos

### Takeaway
The first 30 seconds follow the same rule (confirm the click, no preamble). Long-form adds a **roadmap or stakes beat** and **recurring re-hooks** at section starts (MrBeast's 1–3 and 3–6 minute checkpoints; BINGE micro-hooks). Chapters matter more. Evidence for specific lift numbers is weak.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast memo: distinct goals for minute 1, minutes 1–3 ("crazy progression"), and minutes 3–6 (re-engagement) — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- Long-form hook = extend to 2–4 sentences (pattern interrupt, promise, open loop, stakes). Shorts hooks run under 5s — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)
- BINGE: micro-hooks at each section, and avoid early "wrap-up" signals — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)
- Chapters help viewers navigate and return. Guides suggest ~1 chapter per 4–6 minutes for long videos. Claimed 12–18% retention lifts are unsourced — [InstantViews](https://instantviews.net/youtube-chapters-long-videos)
- Design segments so they can be cut into standalone Shorts — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-retention-strategy-how-to-hook-hold-and-convert-viewers/)

### Inferences
- **6–10 min daily brief:** hook ≤15s (fact plus why it matters), ritual ≤3s, credential ≤5s, then story 1 by ~0:20–0:30. Re-hook every story transition ("Next — and this one affects your paycheck…"). Save one teased story for about 60–70% through.
- **30-min weekend tutorial:** show the end result first (0–10s), state stakes or time saved (10–20s), add a skeptic beat ("this isn't a get-rich scheme; here's exactly what it does and doesn't do"), give a roadmap of steps with chapter markers (20–45s), then a re-hook at each chapter.
- **30-min weekly recap:** lead with the single biggest AI+money story of the week, then a countdown or roadmap ("the 7 stories that mattered, and the one most people missed"). The "one most people missed" is an open loop that pays off in the final third. Use chapters for each story.

### Gaps
- No data found comparing first-30-second retention by video length, or on 30-minute talking-head finance content specifically.

## 8. Credibility conflicts for finance content (hook tactics to avoid)

### Cited Findings
- Legalaes study of 1,764 finance videos: misleading videos averaged ~555,547 views versus ~326,170 for non-misleading ones. Only 2.2% of 1,266 finfluencers had relevant credentials. Only 11.7% of videos carried a disclaimer. The method was rule-based text classification, not manual fact-checking — [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/finfluencer-engagement-is-not-evidence-of-credibility/VgZeyvJlpOEMdjX9); [FA Mag](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/nearly-98--of--finfluencers--lack-relevant-credentials--new-video-analysis-finds-88443.html); [Money.ca](https://money.ca/managing-money/budgeting/finfluencers-financial-advice-qualifications-social-media)
- YouTube had the highest share of misleading finance videos (~41.8–42%) and also the highest share classified as accurate (38.3%). Sources differ slightly on the figure — [Global Relay](https://www.grip.globalrelay.com/few-finfluencers-have-real-financial-qualifications-analysis-finds/); [Yahoo Finance CA](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/only-2-2-financial-content-110000452.html)
- Finance viewers arrive skeptical, so generic clickbait works less well than in entertainment (practitioner view) — [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/finance-youtube-ctr-tips)

### Inferences
- **Hook tactics to ban or flag for this channel:**
  - "The market is about to crash" or prediction-as-fact hooks
  - "Buy this before…" / "This stock will 10x" (also violates the no-recommendation rule)
  - Thumbnail numbers that aren't in the video
  - "BROKE" / "SHOCKING" framing (Wes Roth-style) on unconfirmed AI claims
  - Cold-opening on rumors
- Misleading content *does* get more views (Legalaes), so there is a real tradeoff. But Test & Compare and Ritchie's guidance reward watch time and kept promises, and the channel's long-term asset is daily-habit trust.
- **Credibility-safe hook substitutes:** a specific verified number, "what changed overnight", a myth-correction, "what this means for a normal 401(k)", or "one thing to check today" (educational, not a trade).

### Gaps
- No study found on whether disclaimers or credential lines in the first 30 seconds hurt or help retention for finance content.
