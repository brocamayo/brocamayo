## 1. On-camera delivery: teleprompter vs. memorized vs. bullet points; energy, eye contact, natural reading, early-morning filming, chunks vs. one take

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced creator testimony (Matt D'Avella) favors **writing a full script in your own voice and then leaving room to improvise between scripted beats**, rather than riffing. General guidance pits teleprompter consistency against bullet-point naturalness. The usual compromise is a full script for news facts and numbers, with a looser delivery around them.

### Cited Findings
- Matt D'Avella said his videos got much better once he planned, scripted and wrote them out, compared with riffing to camera. — [Creator Science podcast #21: Matt D'Avella](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/matt-davella/)
- D'Avella writes scripts "in his own voice" and leaves room to improvise in the moment between scripted beats. — [Creator Science podcast #21](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/matt-davella/)
- Ali Abdaal's on-camera style is described as casual and natural, "like you're sitting down for coffee," with a fast pace and lots of B-roll. The source does not say whether he reads from a prompter. — [Riverside: talking head videos](https://marketing.riverside.com/blog/talking-head-videos)
- Teleprompter trade-off, from teleprompter vendors and guides:
  - Reading word by word can sound "robotic and flat."
  - For conversational formats, the same guide suggests switching to bullet points.
  - Prompters do help with eye contact, cut stumbles and keep pacing steady.
  - Vendor sources, moderate bias. — [Teleprompter.com: teleprompter vs traditional script reading](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/teleprompter-vs-traditional-script-reading); [Teleprompter.com: video podcast](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-use-a-teleprompter-for-a-video-podcast)
- Bullet-point delivery sits between a memorized script and improvisation. It uses bullets as cues and is argued to feel more authentic. — [Talk for Impact: Unscripted video mastery](https://talkforimpact.substack.com/p/unscripted-video-mastery-how-to-record)
- A university lecture-video guide finds most presenters prefer talking points because they give "a more natural flow and fewer takes and edits." It still recommends full scripts when no point can be missed. — [UF Pharmacy: Preparing your script for teleprompter](https://video.pharmacy.ufl.edu/resources/user-guides/preparing-your-script-for-teleprompter/)
- Teleprompter technique:
  - Take in whole lines at a time to reduce darting eye movement. — [Devil Boy Productions: look natural on a teleprompter](https://www.devilboyproductions.com/video-news-production-blog/how-to-look-natural-on-a-teleprompter); [PremiumBeat: 7 tips for teleprompters](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/7-video-tips-working-teleprompters/)
  - Rehearse beforehand, because the prompter shows only a small part of the script at once. — [Glide Gear: tips for teleprompters](https://glidegear.net/blogs/news/tips-for-working-with-teleprompters)
  - If every sentence comes out at the same speed and weight, the read feels mechanical. — [Devil Boy Productions](https://www.devilboyproductions.com/video-news-production-blog/how-to-look-natural-on-a-teleprompter)
- Free and cheap teleprompter apps are widely available as of 2026 (e.g., Speakflow, browser-based prompters). — [Speakflow: Complete guide to free online teleprompters (2026)](https://www.speakflow.com/blog/posts/free-online-teleprompter-guide)
- AI eye-contact correction can make a speaker reading a script appear to look into the lens. Descript offers this feature. — [Descript](https://www.descript.com/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended approach for a 6:50am one-take news brief:** use a hybrid.
  - Script the segment headlines, every number, and every "made-up data" label word for word, because numbers are where ad-libbing causes errors.
  - Deliver transitions and the "what this means for your money" commentary from 1–3 bullet cues.
  - Put this in the per-segment production note as `DELIVERY: scripted / bullets`.
- **Eye-line options:**
  - A phone-mounted teleprompter or a beam-splitter prompter keeps the eyes on the lens.
  - Notes on a screen beside the camera do not.
  - If the creator uses notes beside the camera, Descript-style eye-contact correction is a fallback. Test it for an uncanny look before relying on it.
- **"One take" in practice:** treat it as one continuous recording with pickups. If you stumble, pause, then restart the sentence. Remove the bad take with text-based editing (Descript), or with silence and retake detection in other tools (see Section 5). Truly unedited one-takes put pressure on delivery. Restarting a sentence is cheap to edit out.
- **Energy:** match the "coffee with a friend" register (the Abdaal descriptor) rather than broadcast-anchor energy. An early-morning audience is consuming the brief alongside a routine.

## 2. Setup on a budget: camera/phone, framing, lighting, audio, room treatment; budget tiers

### Takeaway
There is near-universal creator and guide consensus that **audio is the first upgrade, lighting the second, and the camera last**. A phone with window light and a $15–$100 mic is a credible starting tier. Ali Abdaal explicitly endorses starting with a phone propped on books.

### Cited Findings
- **Abdaal on starting out:** "As a total beginner, you can get away with using your phone and propping it up on a stack of books." His budget-tier guide covers audio, video and lighting tiers. — [Ali Abdaal: YouTube Gear for Different Budgets](https://aliabdaal.com/youtube/youtube-gear-for-different-budgets/) (via search summary; full page not retrieved)
- **Abdaal's pro-tier gear:**
  - **Older setup (circa 2020–2021):** Sennheiser MKH 416 shotgun mic, Rode VideoMic Pro+, Zoom H5 recorder, Sony cameras. — [Ali Abdaal: My YouTube Gear + Studio Setup](https://aliabdaal.com/?p=782) (dated)
  - **Newer listing (third party):** Sony A7S III, Sony ZV-E1, and two Aputure LS 300X key lights. — [Vlogging Pro: Ali Abdaal setup](https://vloggingpro.com/setups/ali-abdaal/)
  - **Conflict:** the two lists differ, so his current setup is uncertain. It is aspirational context only.
- **Abdaal's studio:**
  - Uses a large window as the primary light source.
  - Shoots into a room corner, which he finds more visually engaging than shooting straight at a flat wall. — [Summify summary of "How I built a YouTube studio in a tiny room"](https://summify.io/discover/how-i-built-a-youtube-studio-in-a-tiny-room-8-WiFv/)
- **Audio vs. video:**
  - Audio is "arguably even more important than video quality."
  - Viewers tolerate weak visuals but leave over bad sound.
  - Source caveat: Subscribr, a YouTube-tool vendor, publishes these guides. One of its pieces cites an "84% of viewers demand clear sound" statistic with no source, so do not use that number. — [Subscribr: Is bad audio killing your channel?](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/improve-youtube-audio-quality); [Subscribr: Pro audio on a budget](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-pro-audio-budget)
- **Lighting over camera:** "The camera itself is often less important than lighting." Face a window for soft, even light. — [Subscribr: Budget YouTube studio setup](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/budget-youtube-studio-setup)
- **Mic tiers:**
  - Wired lavs cost about $15–$20 (Boya BY-M1 is commonly recommended).
  - USB desk mics cost about $50–$100.
  - The right mic depends on whether you film at a desk, on the move, or talking to camera. — [Subscribr: Best budget microphones under $100](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/best-budget-microphones-youtube-beginners); [Uppbeat: microphones for YouTube](https://uppbeat.io/blog/equipment/microphones-for-youtube)
- **Room treatment:** use a quiet room, and soft furnishings to cut echo and reverb. Room treatment matters about as much as the mic. — [Subscribr: How to choose a microphone](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/how-to-choose-microphone-youtube)
- **Post-production audio repair:** Descript "Studio Sound" uses regenerative AI to remove noise and enhance voice in one click. — [Descript](https://www.descript.com/); [Feisworld: Descript Season 6/Underlord recap](https://www.feisworld.com/blog/descript-season6-underlord-recap)

### Inferences
Draft budget tiers for the checklist, built from the cited price points:
- **Tier 0 (~$0–$40):**
  - Phone rear camera at 4K30, propped or on a cheap tripod.
  - Face a window.
  - $15–$20 wired lav plugged into the phone.
  - Soft furnishings in the room.
  - Studio Sound-style cleanup in post.
- **Tier 1 (~$100–$300):** Tier 0 plus:
  - A wireless lav kit or a USB/dynamic mic for the desk.
  - One LED key light with a softbox.
  - A phone teleprompter.
- **Tier 2 (~$800+):**
  - Mirrorless camera with clean HDMI or a webcam mode.
  - Shotgun or broadcast dynamic mic.
  - Key light plus fill.
  - Second angle for cuts.
- **Lighting at 6:50am:** window light is unreliable that early in winter months, and dawn light is also weak and blue. A daily 6:50am shoot therefore argues for **one LED key light as the first non-audio purchase**, so that every episode looks the same.
- **Framing:** use mid-chest-up framing with headroom left for lower-thirds and on-screen numbers. Use a 16:9 frame with enough side margin to crop a 9:16 Short later (see Section 7).

### Gaps
- No primary MKBHD, Think Media or Peter McKinnon gear guide (2023–2026) was retrieved. The "audio > video" consensus rests mainly on vendor blogs plus widespread practitioner consensus, not on controlled data.
- No sourced data was found on phone vs. dedicated-camera viewer perception.

## 3. Editing for retention: jump cuts, punch-ins, B-roll, on-screen text/numbers, charts, sound design, music, captions; what finance/AI channels use

### Takeaway
Cutting dead air (jump cuts) plus subtle punch-ins is the consensus talking-head retention edit. The "something changes every ~3 seconds" cadence is a **trend description from tool vendors, not tested evidence**. Match it to your own audience-retention graph. The leaked MrBeast production handbook adds structural guidance: guard the first minute, and re-engage at fixed intervals.

### Cited Findings
- **Jump cuts:** they "compress your content, removing dead air and keeping viewers through the whole video." YouTube's own audience-retention report shows the exact points where viewers drop. — [1of10: What is a jump cut](https://1of10.com/blog/what-is-a-jump-cut/); [Social Video Plaza: increase retention](https://www.socialvideoplaza.com/en/articles/increase-youtube-audience-retention)
- **Punch-ins:**
  - Cut on a word boundary, and often add a small punch-in or punch-out so the cut reads as a new sentence rather than missing footage. — [Faceless.so: 9 retention editing techniques](https://faceless.so/blog/video-retention-editing-9-techniques-that-work)
  - Zoom speed: fast zooms (3–6 frames) suit high-energy moments; smooth zooms (10–20 frames) suit storytelling. — [Premiere Copilot: The retention edit trap](https://www.premierecopilot.com/blog/auto-zoom-ai-emotion-detection-premiere-pro)
  - The "every three seconds something visually changes" style is described as a trend by the same vendor. It is not a tested rule. — [Premiere Copilot](https://www.premierecopilot.com/blog/auto-zoom-ai-emotion-detection-premiere-pro)
  - Workload: manual punch-ins add up to 40–80 zoom moments in a 10-minute video. That is why AI auto-zoom tools are marketed. — [Premiere Copilot](https://www.premierecopilot.com/blog/auto-zoom-ai-emotion-detection-premiere-pro)
- **Unverified statistic:** "fast-paced editing shows up to 3x higher completion rate" has no study behind it. Do not use it. — [BlitzCut AI: Jump cuts vs cross dissolves](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/jump-cuts-vs-cross-dissolves)
- **Ali Abdaal's style:** fast-moving pace and lots of B-roll. — [Riverside: talking head videos](https://marketing.riverside.com/blog/talking-head-videos)
- **Johnny Harris's style:** blends motion graphics with cinematic footage. This comes from a low-quality aggregator and is only a general characterization. — [JHU Stripe aggregator page](https://stripe.jhu.edu/news/heightened-perspectives-exploring-the-perspective-of-johnny-harris-imposing-stature)
- **MrBeast production handbook** (leaked August 2024; two former producers confirmed it to Passionfruit; MrBeast's spokesperson declined comment):
  - It tracks CTR, average view duration (AVD) and average view percentage (AVP).
  - It treats the first minute as the biggest drop-off point.
  - Per one summary, it places "re-engagement" content around minutes 3 and 6.
  - These are secondary write-ups, and the handbook targets big-budget entertainment, not news briefs. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/); [Creator Handbook: key takeaways](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/); [Alexander Jarvis copy of memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- **Early daily-news editing style (2012):** Philip DeFranco's SourceFed was described as "frenetic edits and an upbeat soundtrack" across multiple single-subject daily videos. This is old, flagged as pre-2023. — [Tubefilter (2012)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2012/01/26/phil-defranco-sourcefed/amp/)

### Inferences
- **Graphics for an 8–10 minute brief:**
  - Each segment gets a headline card.
  - A full-screen graphic or callout every time a number is spoken: price, percent move, dollar figure.
  - An article screenshot with a highlighter box for AI news.
  - These visual changes double as "pattern interrupts," so the creator needs fewer manual punch-ins.
- **Template-first approach:** build 5–6 reusable templates in the editor:
  - Headline card
  - Ticker/number callout
  - Chart frame with a "HYPOTHETICAL DATA" tag
  - Article quote card
  - Lower-third
  - End screen
- **Re-engagement cadence:** apply the MrBeast-style idea at news-segment boundaries. Example: tease the "money move" segment early, then pay it off around minute 5–6. This is an adaptation, not something the handbook recommends for news.
- **Music:** a low music bed under segment transitions (not under dense numeric explanation), plus a consistent sting per segment, works as a cheap brand cue.
  - No sourced evidence was found for this. Treat it as a style choice and test it.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found on how Graham Stephan, Humphrey Yang, Andrei Jikh or Patrick Boyle build their stock charts or graphics.
- No controlled data was found on music beds, sound effects, or burned-in captions vs. CC for long-form retention.
- No official YouTube guidance on edit pace was retrieved.

## 4. Screen-recording tutorials (20–40 minutes)

### Takeaway
The tooling has converged on **automatic cursor-following zoom**: Screen Studio on Mac (~$89 one-time per one review), Descript (subscription), and open-source options such as Open Screen. Core craft rules:
- Scale the UI to 110–125%.
- Move the cursor slowly and pause before and after clicks.
- Keep zooms modest.
- Cut all waits.

### Cited Findings
- **Screen Studio:**
  - Mac-only. Applies auto-zoom, cursor smoothing and motion blur at render time, with no timeline editing needed.
  - Auto-zoom can misfire on fast cursor movement and may need manual keyframe fixes.
  - Cited at $89 one-time. Pricing should be verified on the vendor site. — [Bityclips: Screen Studio vs Descript](https://bityclips.com/compare/screen-studio-vs-descript); [Tooljunction: AI screen recording auto-zoom tools](https://tooljunction.io/blog/ai-screen-recording-auto-zoom-tools); [DevToolLab: Screen Studio](https://devtoollab.com/ai-tools/screen-studio)
- **Descript:**
  - Transcript-based editing, with cursor- and scene-driven smart focus/auto-zoom.
  - Plans from about $12/month (per a comparison blog; verify). — [Tooljunction](https://blog.tooljunction.io/ai-screen-recording-auto-zoom-tools); [Bityclips](https://bityclips.com/compare/screen-studio-vs-descript)
- **Open Screen:** an open-source recorder that auto-suggests zoom regions based on where the cursor paused (covered by Gigazine in April 2026). — [Open Screen docs: creating demos](https://www.mintlify.com/siddharthvaddem/openscreen/guides/creating-demos); [Gigazine (2026-04-17)](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260417-open-screen)
- **Capture settings:**
  - Record at the highest resolution the display allows.
  - 60 fps helps with scrolling and fast cursor movement; 30 fps is fine for most tutorials.
  - Set app or system zoom to 110–125% for readability on small screens.
  - Capture at a 1:1 pixel ratio to avoid HiDPI blur. — [Clueso: Tasteful screen recording – setup](https://www.clueso.io/cluebooks/tasteful-screen-recording--setting-up-screenrecording)
- **Cursor handling:**
  - Move more slowly than feels natural, pause before and after clicks, and use a slightly larger cursor.
  - Use click highlights only where they add something; skip trails and decorative effects. — [Clueso: setup](https://www.clueso.io/cluebooks/making-tasteful-screen-recording-videos--setting-up-screenrecording); [Clueso: visual effects](https://www.clueso.io/cluebooks/tasteful-screen-recording--adding-visual-effects); [Demobites: zoom and cursor](https://explore.demobites.com/en/explore/zoom-and-cursor/)
- **Zoom amount:** keep emphasis zooms modest, around 5–10%, with soft ease-in and ease-out. — [Clueso: adding visual effects](https://www.clueso.io/cluebooks/making-tasteful-screen-recording-videos--adding-visual-effects)

### Inferences
Weekend 30-minute workflow video checklist:
1. Use a clean browser profile with hidden bookmarks and notifications, and fake/demo accounts only. Never show real balances or emails.
2. Set UI zoom to 125%.
3. Use a face-cam bubble in a corner that never covers the UI being taught. Move it, or hide it during dense screens.
4. Record one section per chapter, so restarts are cheap.
5. Cut every load or AI-generation wait to under 1 second, or show a "⏩ sped up" label. A 30-minute target should run about 40–60 minutes raw.
6. Open with a 20–40 second preview of the finished result, so viewers know the payoff.
7. Add YouTube chapters (timestamps in the description) for each workflow step.

Other notes:
- Screen Studio's auto-zoom suits Mac users who want speed.
- On Windows, OBS plus Descript, or an open-source auto-zoom tool, is the cheaper path.

### Gaps
- Primal Video/Justin Brown's own screen-recording guidance was not found.
- Screen Studio and Descript pricing comes only from third-party comparison sites; verify it on vendor pages.
- No source covered recommended on-screen font sizes for tutorials.
- No official YouTube chapters help page was retrieved (the support site was unreachable).

## 5. Fast daily workflows, editing tools and realistic edit times

### Takeaway
Little primary documentation exists on how daily news and market-recap creators (DeFranco, Meet Kevin, etc.) produce episodes. The practical speed lever as of 2025–2026 is AI-assisted editing:
- Text-based edits
- Automatic filler and silence removal
- One-click audio cleanup
- Eye-contact correction
- Auto-captions

Together with fixed graphic templates, these can bring a 10-minute talking-head edit to roughly 1–2 hours (one guide's benchmark) or less.

### Cited Findings
- Some efficient creators edit a 10-minute video in 1–2 hours. This is a general guide with no specific creator named. — [Subscribr: faster YouTube editing workflow](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/faster-youtube-video-editing-workflow)
- **Descript AI features:**
  - Automatic removal of fillers ("um," "uh," "like," "you know").
  - Studio Sound.
  - Eye-contact correction.
  - "Underlord," an AI co-editor/assistant inside the app.
  - Some features were labeled "SOON" on Descript pages, so check the current status. — [Descript: remove filler words](https://www.descript.com/tools/remove-filler-from-audio); [Descript use case: removing ums](https://www.descript.com/use-cases/removing-ums-and-filler-words-from-a-recording); [Descript home](https://www.descript.com/); [Feisworld: Descript Season 6 Underlord recap](https://www.feisworld.com/blog/descript-season6-underlord-recap)
- **Morning-video example:** marketer Christopher S. Penn documented his morning video production process. It is from 2020, so flagged as older, and is a single-creator example. — [Christopher S. Penn: Morning video production process (2020)](https://www.christopherspenn.com/2020/06/you-ask-i-answer-morning-video-production-process-2/)
- **DeFranco:**
  - Historically the brand had production support (Revision3, 2012).
  - The style was frenetic edits with an upbeat soundtrack.
  - No current workflow detail was found. — [Tubefilter (2012)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2012/01/26/phil-defranco-sourcefed/amp/)

### Inferences
Proposed 6:50am daily pipeline, about 90–120 minutes start to publish:

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| Night before / 6:00 | Script finalized; graphics placeholders filled from template (headline cards, made-up-data charts) |
| 6:40 | Light on, mic check, 30-second record-and-playback test |
| 6:50 | Record continuous take with clap/pause pickups (~10–14 min raw) |
| ~7:05 | Import into Descript or similar; remove fillers and silences; delete retakes in the transcript |
| | Studio Sound / noise cleanup |
| | Drop in pre-built graphics at script markers; auto-captions |
| ~8:15 | Export; upload with a templated title/description/disclaimer |
| ~8:30 | Publish; cut 1–2 Shorts after |

Tool choices:
- Descript is strongest for text-based speed.
- CapCut is cheap/free and fast for Shorts.
- DaVinci Resolve is free but slower to learn.
- Premiere/Riverside are optional.
- Pick one editor and template it rather than switching between tools.

Per-segment production notes in each script could use fixed fields:
- `DELIVERY`
- `ON-SCREEN TEXT`
- `GRAPHIC/CHART (label: hypothetical)`
- `B-ROLL/SCREENSHOT`
- `SFX/MUSIC`
- `SHORT CANDIDATE? (Y/N + timestamp)`

## 6. Thumbnails & titles; YouTube "Test & Compare"

### Takeaway
YouTube's native Test & Compare tests up to three variants and picks a winner by **watch time share, not CTR**. It now covers titles as well as thumbnails, with a broad rollout reported in late 2025. Tests need distinct variants and enough impressions, so a small channel will often see "no winner." Make the variants meaningfully different.

### Cited Findings
- **Watch time share:** the tool evaluates thumbnails by watch time share. YouTube says this "will best support creators' growth." Third-party tools often optimize for CTR and can pick a different winner. — [YouTube Help: Test & compare thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714) (via search summary)
- **Result labels:**
  - "Winner": statistically significant.
  - "Preferred": likely better, but not certain.
  - "None": no meaningful difference.
  - If there is no clear winner, the first uploaded thumbnail stays as the default.
  - Results appear in Studio, with a report in Analytics → Reach. — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- **Timing:** results take from a few days to about two weeks. Diversity between thumbnails and impression volume both affect speed. Common reasons for no winner: the thumbnails were too similar, or there were too few impressions. — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- **Eligibility:**
  - Needs advanced features and desktop Studio.
  - Works for public long-form videos, archived lives and podcast episodes.
  - Not for private videos, made-for-kids videos, or mature content. — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- **Titles:** a newer help page covers A/B testing titles as well as thumbnails.
  - Label conflict: one source shows "Performed Same" instead of "None" as the no-difference label.
  - Vidanalyze describes a December 2025 global rollout.
  - Defer to the official page for current labels. — [YouTube Help: A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400); [Vidanalyze: YouTube A/B testing 2026 guide](https://vidanalyze.com/news/youtube-ab-testing-for-titles-thumbnails-2026-guide)
- **General rollout coverage:** [Fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/426241/youtube-thumbnail-test-compare-tool-all-creators.html/amp); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/youtube-thumbnail-ab-testing_id159318); [Toptal creator post on thumbnail A/B testing](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-thumbnail-ab-testing)
- **Metrics to track:** the MrBeast handbook names CTR, AVD and AVP as the three core metrics. This is consistent with YouTube weighing post-click watch time. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/)

### Inferences
- For a daily news brief:
  - Thumbnails decay fast, and a test can take days to two weeks, so A/B testing each daily episode has limited value.
  - Use Test & Compare on the **weekend evergreen tutorials and recaps**.
  - For dailies, use a consistent branded template (face + date/series badge + 2–4 word hook + one visual such as a ticker arrow or AI logo) and iterate across episodes instead.
- Test meaningfully different concepts (face vs. no face, number vs. word, chart vs. product logo), not color tweaks. Tests between similar thumbnails tend to return no winner.

### Gaps
- No sourced data was found on finance- or AI-specific thumbnail findings (face vs. no face, word counts, red/green arrows).
- No creator write-ups (e.g., from Colin and Samir or Film Booth) of what they learned from Test & Compare were retrieved.
- Mobile-readability rules (word count, font size) are unsourced in this research.

## 7. Shorts from long videos

### Takeaway
Shorts can officially run up to **3 minutes** (square or vertical uploads made after October 15, 2024). Third-party guides still argue that shorter performs better on retention, but that is opinion, not official guidance.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Shorts can be up to three minutes. Uploads must be square or vertical. The rule applies to videos uploaded after October 15, 2024; earlier uploads stay long-form. — [YouTube Help: Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- The rollout was phased; all users had 3-minute creation tools after "Phase 4." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/)
- **Length:**

## 8. Compliance & visual credibility for finance (sources on screen, labeling made-up data, disclaimers)

### Takeaway
Disclaimers are common: in one study, 73% of finance YouTubers posted them, and they were associated with moderated market reactions. Regulators and legal scholars say **a disclaimer does not by itself turn a recommendation into "not advice."** A disclaimer flashed only briefly has been criticized in at least one enforcement context (India's SEBI). For made-up data, the safest practice is a persistent on-screen label plus a verbal statement.

### Cited Findings
- **Study:** 73% of financial YouTubers studied post disclaimers. Researchers found "moderated market responses to videos with disclaimers." — [Mark Bradshaw (UTS): Financial YouTube channels and the capital markets (2024 PDF)](https://drupal.uts.edu.au/sites/default/files/2024-01/Mark%20Bradshaw%20-%20Financial%20YouTube%20Channels%20and%20the%20Capital%20Markets.pdf)
- **Legal view (NZ):** "the mere inclusion of a disclaimer will not necessarily carve a finfluencer's statement out of the definition of 'financial advice'." The test is how a reasonable observer reads it. — [New Zealand Law Review 2024, issue 3](https://www.legalresearch.org.nz/new-zealand-law-review/published-issues/2024/issue-3.aspx)
- **India (SEBI):** the disclaimer "can't be a defense" where assured returns are promised. The case discussed involved disclaimers flashed for milliseconds at the start of a video. This is a forum summary of the order, not the order itself. — [TradingQnA: SEBI order against YouTube F&O tipster](https://tradingqna.com/t/sebi-order-against-a-youtube-f-o-tipster/108376)
- **US disclosure risk:** the SEC's 2022 settlement with Kim Kardashian ($1.26M over an undisclosed $250K crypto promotion payment) is the cited example. It is relayed by syndicated news, not the SEC order. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/youtube-star-mrbeast-planning-financial-160000177.html); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/finance/youtube-megastar-mrbeast-planning-financial-160000357.html)
- **Advocacy:** Better Markets published an April 2026 fact sheet on finfluencers in connection with MrBeast's finance plans. It shows continued regulatory and advocacy attention in 2026. — [Better Markets fact sheet (2026-04-14)](https://bettermarkets.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Fact-Sheet_MrBeast-Finfluencers_4-14-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
Production rules for this channel. These are practical and not legal advice; verify with official SEC/FINRA/FTC guidance.
- **Made-up data:**
  - Every chart and number card with made-up data carries a persistent corner tag: "HYPOTHETICAL / ILLUSTRATIVE DATA – not real prices."
  - Say "these are made-up numbers for illustration" aloud the first time made-up data appears in each video.
  - A label that stays on screen is stronger than a flashed one, given the SEBI critique.
- **Sources:**
  - Show the source on screen whenever real AI news is cited: outlet name + date in a lower-third, or an article screenshot with the URL visible.
  - This also builds credibility.
- **Disclaimer:**
  - Use a standing verbal and on-screen line ("educational, not financial advice") in the intro or outro, with the full text in the description.
  - Avoid "guaranteed" or return-promising language in scripts, since a disclaimer does not cure it.
- **Sponsorships:** disclose any sponsorship or affiliate link verbally and with YouTube's paid-promotion checkbox (the Kardashian precedent).

- No source specifically addressed labeling fabricated or hypothetical market data in videos.
- The YouTube paid-promotion disclosure help page was not retrieved; the support site was unreachable.
